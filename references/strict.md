# Strict-mode notes

Read this file only when the user asks for strict, full, or 100% STE.

## Limits

ASD-STE100 is an English controlled-language standard with writing rules and a dictionary.
This skill does not bundle the standard or its dictionary. Model recall is not an authoritative dictionary lookup.

- Use the issue and reference material supplied by the user.
- If the required reference is missing, give a strict-oriented draft with a brief verification caveat.
- Do not fabricate approved words, meanings, rule numbers, or a compliance score.
- Do not claim that a draft is certified or verified from these notes alone.
- For safety-critical documentation, recommend review against the applicable standard by a qualified reviewer.

## Draft checklist

These are drafting reminders, not a substitute for the full standard:

1. Keep procedural sentences at 20 words or fewer and descriptive sentences at 25 words or fewer, subject to the applicable counting rules.
2. Use the imperative for instructions. Put one instruction in each step.
3. Make conditions explicit before the action. Separate distinct actions.
4. Avoid contractions and Latin abbreviations.
5. Do not omit required articles or `that`.
6. Avoid `-ing` verb forms. Check exceptions against the applicable standard rather than banning every word that ends in those letters.
7. Check each word's approved meaning and part of speech. Validate technical nouns and technical verbs against the applicable rules.
8. Preserve all source facts, limits, and literal content. If a style rule conflicts, keep the meaning and flag the conflict.

## Safety text

Preserve the source's signal word, hazard, consequence, and avoidance action.
Do not downgrade a WARNING to a CAUTION or invent a software-specific hazard classification.
If the source has no classification, use a plain risk statement and ask for the applicable safety convention when a formal label is necessary.
Never invent a precaution and present it as an approved procedure.

Official reference: [ASD-STE100](https://www.asd-ste100.org/).
